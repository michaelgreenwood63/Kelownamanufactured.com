Meadowbrook Estates

720 Commonwealth Road, Lake Country

Duck Lake Indian Reserve No. 7

⚠️ The most important fact about this park: the head lease expires in December 2026 — a genuinely near-term date. The park manager has told sellers they'll continue to operate, but that's not the same as a confirmed long-term lease renewal. Every other reserve-land park in our directory has a documented lease running decades further out (2062 to 2151) — this one doesn't. If you're considering a purchase here, confirming the current, actual lease status directly with the Okanagan Indian Band's Lands Department or a lawyer isn't optional — it's the single most important thing to verify before you buy.

Whose land is this on?

About Duck Lake Indian Reserve No. 7: this park sits on reserve land administered by the Okanagan Indian Band — a completely different First Nation and reserve from Westbank First Nation in West Kelowna. We have not found evidence the Okanagan Indian Band has passed its own dedicated residential tenancy law the way WFN has; protections here likely depend more heavily on the specific lease or sublease document and general Indian Act leasing provisions. Confirm directly with the Okanagan Indian Band's Lands Department or a lawyer before relying on any assumption. See our full explanation.

What we know beyond the lease situation

Pad rent on recent listings runs around $540 a month. One listing describes the community as having a family section, suggesting a mix of age-restricted and all-ages areas similar to what we've seen at Westview Village in West Kelowna.

We're not telling you not to buy here — plenty of reserve-land leases get renewed, and the park manager's reassurance may well prove accurate. But a near-term lease date is a fundamentally different risk profile than a lease running to 2114 or 2151, and it deserves a direct, current answer from the band's Lands Department before you commit, not an assumption either way.

This page provides general information and is not legal advice. Park policies, pad rent, and lease terms can change — please confirm current details with the park and a lawyer or notary before relying on them.
