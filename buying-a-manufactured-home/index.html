Buying a Manufactured Home

A handful of specific checks before you make an offer — most of which a typical home purchase doesn't require at all.

The short version: buying a manufactured home in a leasehold park involves several checks a typical freehold home purchase doesn't — who owns the land, whether a lender will actually finance it, what the park's rules are, and whether the home itself meets the construction standard it's supposed to. Skip these and you can end up with an accepted offer you can't actually close.

1. Confirm the land jurisdiction

Is the park on Westbank First Nation land, Duck Lake Indian Reserve No. 7, regular BC provincial land, or — rarely — land you'd own outright? Each has different rules for pad rent, assignment, and what happens when a lease term runs out. See our full breakdown and check the specific park in our directory.

2. Confirm financing is actually available

This is the step that catches the most buyers off guard. A number of parks we track only accept specific lenders — Peace Hills Trust is the most common example on WFN land — or require cash purchases entirely. Get pre-approval confirmed for the specific park, not just a general manufactured-home mortgage pre-approval, before you make an offer. See our financing guide for why this happens.

3. Check the compliance plate — is it a manufactured home or modular home?

Factory-built homes in Canada are certified under one of a few different standards, and the difference matters for insurance, financing, and resale. A CSA Z240 compliance plate means it's a manufactured home — a stand-alone building code specific to factory-built homes on a steel frame, not tied to the BC Building Code. A CSA A277 label means it's a modular home — built to full BC Building Code compliance in a factory, which some lenders and insurers treat more like a conventional stick-built home. See our guide to the difference for what this actually changes for you as a buyer.

4. Check pad rent and what's included

Pad rent varies widely even within the same area — anywhere from under $500/month to over $800/month. Confirm what's actually included (water, sewer, garbage are common; some parks bill these separately) and whether pad rent typically jumps after a sale, which happens at several parks in our directory.

5. Check the park's rules before you fall in love with the home

Pet policies, age restrictions, and rental permissions vary park to park, sometimes significantly. If you have a dog over 30 pounds, or plan to rent the home out occasionally, or need an all-ages community rather than 55+, confirm this against the specific park — not a general impression of "mobile home parks" — before you get attached to a listing.

6. Ask about the remaining lease term

On leasehold land, ask directly what the remaining lease term is and what's known about renewal. Most WFN leases run many decades out, but at least one reserve-land park has a lease expiring within a year or two of this writing — a materially different risk profile.

Common Questions

Buying a Manufactured Home — FAQ

What's the difference between a manufactured home and a modular home?
A manufactured home (CSA Z240 certified) is built to its own stand-alone factory-built-home code, on a steel frame, and isn't tied to the BC Building Code. A modular home (CSA A277 certified) is built in a factory to full BC Building Code compliance — generally viewed more favourably by some lenders and insurers. See our full guide for details.
Why is financing harder for a manufactured home?
Many manufactured homes sit on leased land rather than land the owner owns outright, which makes lenders more cautious. Some parks we track only accept specific lenders, or require cash purchases entirely — confirm this for the specific park before offering.
What should I check before making an offer on a manufactured home?
The land jurisdiction (WFN, Duck Lake IR#7, BC provincial, or freehold), whether financing is actually available for that specific park, the home's CSA compliance plate type, current pad rent and what it includes, the park's pet/rental/age rules, and the remaining lease term.
Do all manufactured home parks have the same rules?
No — pet policies, age restrictions, rental permissions, pad rent, and even which lenders will finance a home vary significantly park to park, even within the same neighbourhood. Always confirm against the specific park.
