Pad Rent Rules

Pad rent isn't unlimited — but the rules depend entirely on what land your park sits on.

The short version: on regular BC provincial land, pad rent increases are capped and require formal notice. On reserve land — Westbank First Nation or Duck Lake Indian Reserve No. 7 — different rules apply, set by the First Nation or the park operator rather than the province. Knowing which applies to your park changes what a "normal" pad rent increase actually looks like.

BC provincial land: capped increases, 3 months' notice

Under BC's Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act, a park operator must give tenants at least 3 full months' written notice before a rent increase takes effect. Annual increases are capped at a limit set by the province — tied to inflation, plus allowances for the park owner's rising costs or capital expenditures in some circumstances. The exact cap is set annually and published by BC's Residential Tenancy Branch; confirm the current year's figure directly with them or with us, since it changes.

Reserve land: a different framework entirely

On Westbank First Nation land, pad rent and rent-increase rules fall under WFN's own Residential Premises Law, not BC's provincial cap. On Duck Lake Indian Reserve No. 7, we haven't found evidence of the Okanagan Indian Band having passed an equivalent dedicated law — which means pad rent terms there likely come down to the specific lease or sublease document and the park operator's own practice, not a published provincial-style cap. See our full jurisdiction breakdown for more on this distinction.

What we've actually seen in pad rent, park by park

Across the parks we track, pad rent has ranged from under $500/month to over $850/month — and it's genuinely not predictable from the park's general reputation or age alone. Several listings we've reviewed note that pad rent increases specifically upon a change of ownership — meaning the rate a seller is currently paying may not be what a buyer will actually pay once the home changes hands. Always confirm the post-sale pad rent with the park directly, not just what's listed for the current owner.

Practical takeaway: don't assume a rent increase you've heard about is "illegal" without first confirming which jurisdiction your specific park falls under — the rules genuinely aren't the same everywhere in this market, even between two parks a few blocks apart.

Common Questions

Pad Rent Rules — FAQ

Is there a cap on pad rent increases in BC?
For parks on regular BC provincial land, yes — BC's Manufactured Home Park Tenancy Act sets an annual increase limit and requires at least 3 full months' written notice before an increase takes effect. The exact cap is set and published annually.
Do pad rent caps apply on Westbank First Nation land?
No — WFN's own Residential Premises Law governs pad rent there instead of BC's provincial cap.
Does pad rent go up when a home is sold?
Often, yes — several parks we track explicitly note that pad rent increases for a new owner compared to what the current owner pays. Confirm the post-sale rate with the park directly before finalizing a purchase.
How much notice does a park have to give before raising pad rent?
On BC provincial land, at least 3 full months' written notice is required. On reserve land, notice requirements depend on the specific park's practice and, where applicable, the First Nation's own framework.
