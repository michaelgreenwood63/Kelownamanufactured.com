Financing a Manufactured Home

Leasehold land, older homes, and lender caution combine to make this genuinely different from financing a typical house.

The short version: financing a manufactured home is genuinely harder than financing a typical house, mainly because most of these homes sit on leased land rather than land the owner actually owns. Lenders treat that very differently, and a sizeable number of parks in this market restrict financing to a handful of specific lenders — or none at all.

Chattel loan vs. real property mortgage

This is the core distinction. A real property mortgage — the normal kind used for a typical house — covers both the structure and the land underneath it. A chattel loan treats the home as personal property, separate from the land, because you don't own the land — you're paying pad rent for it. Most homes in leasehold manufactured home parks are financed this way, if they're financed at all.

Chattel loans generally come with shorter terms and higher interest rates than a conventional mortgage. If a manufactured home is permanently affixed to its own owned parcel — the freehold situations we cover, like Pinewoods Villa — a conventional real property mortgage is usually possible instead, which is a real financial advantage of that ownership structure.

The home's age matters as much as the land

Separately from land tenure, the age and construction standard of the home itself is a major financing factor. June 15, 1976 is the real watershed date — that's when federal CSA manufactured-home construction standards took effect, and homes built before it generally face the steepest barriers to financing and insurance, often becoming effectively un-mortgageable without a current CSA compliance plate. Separately, most mainstream lenders also apply a rolling age cap of roughly 20-25 years regardless of build date — meaning a home that was easily financeable a decade ago can become harder to finance today purely because it's aged past that window. Don't rely on "the home is from the 1990s, that should be fine" — check the actual compliance plate and ask the lender directly what their current cutoff is.

Lender restrictions, park by park

Across the parks we track, we've repeatedly seen listings name Peace Hills Trust as the only, or one of very few, lenders willing to finance a home in a specific WFN-land park. We've also seen parks that are cash-purchase only, with financing unavailable at all for at least part of the community (Westgate's older southwest section is a clear example). This varies park to park and sometimes even within a single park — always confirm current lender options for the specific park and unit before writing an offer, not based on a general assumption about manufactured home financing.

Credit score requirements for park approval

Separately from mortgage approval, many parks also run their own approval process for incoming residents, often with a minimum credit score requirement — we've seen 700-730 referenced repeatedly across both reserve-land and BC-land parks. This is a park-level gate on top of whatever your lender requires, not a substitute for it.

Why the compliance plate matters for financing too

The CSA certification on your home's compliance plate can affect how a lender and insurer treat it. See our guide to manufactured vs. modular vs. stick-built homes for how CSA Z240 (manufactured) differs from CSA A277 (modular) and why some lenders view them differently.

Practical takeaway: get pre-approval confirmed for the specific park and home before you make an offer, not a general manufactured-home pre-approval. We flag known lender restrictions per park wherever we have them — see the park directory.

Common Questions

Financing a Manufactured Home — FAQ

What's the difference between a chattel loan and a mortgage for a manufactured home?
A real property mortgage covers the home and the land it sits on. A chattel loan covers only the home as personal property, used when the land is leased rather than owned — the common situation in manufactured home parks. Chattel loans generally have shorter terms and higher rates than conventional mortgages.
Why do some parks only accept one lender?
Lenders are cautious about leasehold land, especially reserve land with its own legal framework — some simply won't finance homes there at all. We've repeatedly seen Peace Hills Trust named as the primary or only lender willing to finance homes in specific WFN-land parks.
Is it easier to get a mortgage on a freehold manufactured home?
Generally yes — if you own the land outright (as at Pinewoods Villa, for example) rather than leasing a pad, a conventional real property mortgage is usually possible instead of a chattel loan.
Is there an age cutoff for financing a manufactured home?
Yes, two separate ones. Homes built before June 15, 1976 — when federal CSA construction standards took effect — are the hardest to finance or insure at all. Separately, most mainstream lenders also apply a rolling cap of roughly 20-25 years of age regardless of build date, so a home's financeability can change over time even if nothing else about it does.
Do manufactured home parks have their own approval requirements too?
Often yes — separate from mortgage approval, many parks run their own buyer approval process, frequently including a minimum credit score (700-730 is common in our research).
